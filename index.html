The idea

Sustainability budgets are hard to defend. A penny isn't.

Nature funding usually has to survive a budget round, a new finance director and a bad quarter. Penny for Nature sits somewhere far more durable: your cost of goods.

It scales with the thing you already care about most — units out of the door. A good year funds more land. A quiet year funds less. Nobody has to argue for it again.

And because it is tied to a real unit of sale, it produces the one thing marketing teams can never get from a donation: a number your customers can hold in their heads.

How it works

Three steps, then it runs itself.

  1. Pick your unit and your rate

    A penny per bottle, per pair, per box, per booking. You choose the unit of sale and the rate, and we agree a floor and a cap so finance knows the range before you sign.

  2. The money buys land into management

    Contributions are pooled quarterly and committed to specific parcels of British land — peatland, native woodland, species-rich grassland — secured under long-term legal agreements rather than annual grants.

  3. You get the parcel, the data and the story

    Named sites with grid references, baseline and annual ecological survey data, photography, and a visit for your team. Everything you need for a report, a pack shot or a shelf-edge claim.

Saying it safely

Built so your legal team says yes.

The CMA's Green Claims Code has made most brands nervous about talking about nature at all. Penny for Nature is deliberately structured around what you can substantiate.

  • A specific action, not an offsetNo carbon accounting, no netting off, no claim about your footprint. Money in, land restored.
  • Named land, not a portfolioYou can point at a place on a map, which is the difference between a claim and a story.
  • Additional by constructionSites are ones that would not otherwise have come into restoration, and we document why.
  • Auditable in one lineUnits sold × your rate. Your own sales ledger is the evidence base.

Questions we get first

The practical bits.

Is there a minimum?

Yes — enough to bring a meaningful parcel into management rather than fund a gesture. In practice that means brands selling upwards of a few hundred thousand units a year, or smaller brands choosing a higher rate per unit.

How is it paid?

Quarterly in arrears against your own sales figures, with an agreed annual floor and cap. No upfront commitment and nothing held on account.

Can we put it on the packaging?

That is the point. You get a substantiation pack written for the Green Claims Code, plus approved wording and marks. We would rather review your artwork than read about it later.

What happens to the land afterwards?

Sites are secured under long-term legal agreements — typically a conservation covenant or an equivalent management agreement — so the restoration outlives the partnership.

Is this carbon or biodiversity credits?

Neither. Credits are a market instrument you buy and retire against a target. This is funding for restoration, deliberately kept outside that framework so it stays simple to explain and safe to claim.
